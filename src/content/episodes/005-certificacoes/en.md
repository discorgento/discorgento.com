João Victor Pereira has worked as a Magento developer for about three years, and he talks with Jonatan Machado, Maria Ferro and Ivan Augusto about **Adobe Commerce certifications**. He earned Professional Developer and Expert Front-End Developer almost back to back, even though he did not work directly on front end. His prep was hands-on: in his first months in the field he traded his free time for building modules on his own, and he used Adobe's official practice tests to understand the format.

He walks through the exams: multiple choice with nearly identical code snippets, XML trick questions and questions that build on one another. He also describes the proctored online exam protocol, with a webcam sweep of the room, an open microphone, software that blocks tab switching and an argument, in English, because the proctor demanded a passport. His main advice is to make sure your English reading is solid and not to walk in thinking you must pass. On cost, he mentions about 225 dollars for the Expert exam, year-end promotions, the Adobe Partner discount and renewal every two years, which **is now free**.

The conversation also covers João's winding path: law school, a math teaching degree, an internship on Magento 2.1, teaching at a school in Brusque and a board game store he opened with his brother and sold at the start of the pandemic, before returning to programming as a junior developer. At the end he discusses Magento's uncertain moment after Adobe's letter to the community, his advice to start with the entry-level certifications, and the unrealistic deadlines that sales teams sign off on.
